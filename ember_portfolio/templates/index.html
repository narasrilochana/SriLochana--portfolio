LPG Gas Leakage Detector

Problem statement

LPG is flammable, and a leak that goes unnoticed in a kitchen or storage area is a safety risk. People often cannot detect a small leak early enough on their own.

Objective

Detect an LPG gas leak and provide an alert as soon as gas is detected.

Working principle

A gas-sensing element responds to gas in the surrounding air. The circuit compares that response with a set limit, and when the limit is crossed it switches on the alert.

Components and methodology

The design combines a gas sensor, a stage that decides when the reading is too high, an alert output and a power supply.

Balancing Chemical Equations Using Matrices

Matrix representation

Give each substance an unknown coefficient. Each element becomes a row of a matrix, and each substance becomes a column holding its atom count. Products carry a negative sign so that, for every element, atoms in equal atoms out.

Homogeneous equations

Balancing means solving A·x = 0. The system is homogeneous, so the answer is a family of solutions. We choose the smallest whole-number set.

Row operations

Row operations reduce the matrix to a simpler form, which exposes a free variable. Setting it to 1 and scaling to whole numbers gives the coefficients.

Conservation of atoms and mass

Atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a reaction, so each element must balance on both sides. That is exactly what each row of the matrix enforces.

Example: photosynthesis

For x₁ CO₂ + x₂ H₂O → x₃ C₆H₁₂O₆ + x₄ O₂, the rows give:

C:  x₁ − 6x₃ = 0
H:  2x₂ − 12x₃ = 0
O:  2x₁ + x₂ − 6x₃ − 2x₄ = 0

Taking x₃ = 1 gives x₁ = 6, x₂ = 6 and x₄ = 6, so the balanced equation is 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. This is a worked example of the method; no laboratory experiments were performed.
